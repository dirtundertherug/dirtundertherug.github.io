Seeking the Thoughtful People

As if now were the fin-de-siècle friendly Internet.

By impurify at dirtundertherug.com (LibrePGP 0x666BE6DF7F1093CD).

In the Age of Social Media, the Net is a wasteland. The Net’s New Normal is to be cliquish, paranoid, and rude anywhere outside the boundaries of corporate walled gardens and their open-source clones. It is my recent experience that outside of controlled channels, humans on the Net can’t connect anymore.

It was not always thus. I know it’s my own fault that by mere inattention many years ago, I lost the best and most loyal friend I’ve ever had: A random stranger who lived on another continent, with whom I first struck up a casual e-mail correspondence after crossing paths in a public discussion venue. That seed grew to long-form e-letters, then real friendship. It may be an extreme case, but I think everyone who experienced the Internet at that time must have some similar stories!

I am uninterested in meaningless chit-chat just to kill time—and even less interested in virtual “friends” who will never in any circumstance be real friends in any meaningful sense. If either of those describes your way of doing things, then please save both of us the time, and don’t email me. However, if you’re a thoughtful human who sees the Net as a vast open range for potentially finding likeminded people, then—so am I. When thoughtful people strike up a casual, but serious-minded discussion, who knows where it may lead?

—Well, that’s also a long way of saying: Don’t be shy. If you are a human acting in good faith, it won’t bother me if you drop me a note.
