Judges offload all sorts of work. To some degree—to some degree, that’s both good and necessary; competent leadership involves knowing what to delegate to (human!) subordinates. But of course, it can be problematic.

When I referred to clerks in my other comment, my first thought was of the SCOTUS cert pool. If you’re a clerk in the U.S. Supreme Court’s cert pool, you’re in a prime position to impose your own biases on U.S. Constitutional law and precedent. The short summary that you prepare of a complex case influences the largely opaque decision of whether or not the Court will even hear the case.

Uh-oh. Can you imagine what would (will?) happen if (when?) the cert pool is replaced by an “artificial intelligence” summarizer? I’m shocked that these summarizers are used anywhere in jurisprudence.

More generally, judges’ clerks have all sorts of potential influence. But at least, they are humans—highly educated humans, actually intelligent humans, who are supposed to have ethics. I’m the last person who would idealize such ethical standards; however, a human who should have human ethics is strictly superior to a stochastic parrot that cannot have ethics the way that humans do.

Need I mention that there’s been a ton of research on “AI” bias: Racial bias, gender bias, whatever biases are in the model’s training data. I suppose that’s only of concern when the “AI” doesn’t just falsely claim that someone is a convicted sex offender.
