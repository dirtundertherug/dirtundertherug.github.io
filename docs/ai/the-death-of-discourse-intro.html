So-called “AI” is the death of human discourse.

By impurify (me at impurify.com, LibrePGP 0x666BE6DF7F1093CD).
Published: 2026-10-07
Version: v1.0.0

Imagine a school where cheating is allowed. Every student can always get an easy A, just by cheating. The first and most obvious effect is that grades are worthless: By a process of inflation akin to pumping counterfeit currency into an economy, a report card marked with straight As isn’t worth the paper it’s written on.

As if that’s not bad enough, there is a subtler, more pernicious effect.

Suppose there is a student with an impaired body and a brilliant mind, who is endued with an insatiate curiosity, a passion for knowledge—a hunger not for a card that says “A”, but for the substance that is recognized by earning a grade A. They choose not to cheat, because they want to learn. They enjoy studying, they work hard—and with the strength of their mind, they find solace from the pains and limitations of their disability. Their body is a prison—but in the realm of the mind, they are free. They aspire to do, and to achieve whatever they can with their mind, notwithstanding their body’s limitations. In this aspect, they take as exemplary the life of the late British physicist and mathematician, Professor Stephen Hawking CH CBE FRS FRSA.

Now, along comes a stereotypical bully: A hateful, deeply insecure type of personality, who feels an need to tear others down. The bully kicks puppies and throws rocks at old people. They’d relish beating up a cripple, too—but now, the “cheating allowed” policy has given them an even more vicious possibility.

All that they need to do is to claim that the straight-A crippled student is a cheater.

In the old days, when cheating was strictly forbidden, the allegation may have met skepticism. But now, many folks will just assume it’s true. So, why is that pathetic little cripple pretending to be oh, so smart, when they’re just cheating? They’re a fraud—and they must be the dumbest student in the class!

The disabled student’s talents and hard work are now socially worthless. Their every achievement is negated before they reach it. The realm of the mind, where they once felt so free, has been laid waste.

Such is now the prospect for the written word, for human discourse, and for human society as a whole. In the face of LLMs, the technology behind so-called “generative AI”, any fool can easily procure large quantities of text covering a vast breadth of knowledge—and can pass it off as their own work. The text is of poor quality, and the “knowledge” is unreliable; thus, human language has rapidly developed “AI” senses of the words “slop” and “hallucinations”. In practice, such problems don’t stop the bully from extinguishing the A student with malicious false accusations.

A great many humans hate to read slop. Who wants to waste the effort of their human brain reading low-quality, unreliable text extruded from a dumb machine? When a malicious party wants to tear down a smart writer who as yet lacks an established reputation, all that they need to do is to defame them as dishing out slop. It is a fraudulent accusation not only of fraud, but that one is a fraud. It is similar to a false accusation of plagiarism—but stickier, easier to make, and harder to disprove. The bare accusation deters people from reading, so no one will ever notice the writer’s intelligence and their human qualities; it renders them invisible, and starves them of attention.

For an aspiring writer of poor circumstances, who has nothing but a talent for words, who by right should stand or fall on their own human merits, it would be lethal.

Worst of all for any human, it is literally dehumanizing. And worst of all for humanity, such social conditions will foreseeably dissolve the fabric of human discourse.

I don’t know where U.K. law stands on “AI” issues. (I do know that U.K. libel law is not only strict, but from an American perspective, problematically so.) In the United States, whence I write, lawyers such as Matthew Butterick and his co-counsel at the Saveri Law Firm have been fighting “AI” and its social harms with novel arguments of copyright law, in cases of first impression. But that is only one possible angle on protecting a part of the interests of authors, artists, computer programmers, and other creative people who are in some way already established. It does nothing to protect new creatives from the negation of their talents by false accusations, and by the general devaluation of human talent.

New directions are needed in human society’s response to “AI”.

For we stand now at a juncture where, alas, I must declare explicitly that each and every word of this short essay was produced exclusively by my human brain—with absolutely no LLM usage whatsoever. As recently as four years ago, such a declaration would have been unnecessary; ten years ago, it would have been insane. Now, some people won’t even believe it. Such is the society of the classroom where whether or not it’s formally allowed, cheating is tacitly accepted as commonplace, and even in some degree normalized at the behest of powerful monied interests.
