“AI” Vampires and the Death of Texts

By impurify at dirtundertherug.com (LibrePGP 0x666BE6DF7F1093CD).
Published: 2026-10-01
“Ceci n’est pas une fille (de joie).” — impurify (2024; unpublished), in reference to an image not hereby shown.

I’m an hermeneutical fan of reading a text “within its four corners”. My focus on internal evidence is meant not to exclude human authors, but in part the better to search for them. For personal reasons, I live my life almost entirely through the written word, and I have for years. Thus well have I learnt: A text in itself is a lens through which to gaze upon the human behind it, without the prejudices of what we believe we know about that human.

Words can lie, just as humans can lie. Nevertheless, the text can belie truth in the quest not only for abstract meaning, but also for a meeting of the minds, for a human connection—for not merely authorship, but authenticity. And ultimately, we can do no other: All media of human communication ineluctably encapsulate humans as their representations in texts, a word which in formal usage includes paintings, photographs, videos, elocution, dance, expressive conduct generally, and music.

Texts are all we have—arguably, in final account, all we are. If an atheist may realize a mythopoetic concept, then as seen through the lens of humanity-as-culture, one’s texts are one’s soul.

Case in point: Without the external evidence of knowing that I’ve been consciously influenced by the post-structuralists, an academic1 concisely summarized my thoughts from my texts. “That’s pretty much the insight of the post-structuralists—that in the end, we’re all stuck as texts to be read or misread. You can believe in reality, as I do, and still get that’s true.” Albeit somewhat negative in tone, this interpretation of my text’s internal evidence, my soul thus bared, is consistent with the external evidence of my thoughts.

And it was the reality, before vampires became real.

Artificial Exoparasitoids

A vampire is a mythical exoparasitoid. It lives forever by drinking the blood of humans—draining their life force into itself, for to make itself immortal.

An LLM is a vampire: An artificial exoparasitoid that absorbs human souls, thus draining the creative life-force of culture.

This label does not anthropomorphize machines: A vampire is by definition not only inhuman, but anti-human. Nor does it vivify them: A vampire is typically constructed as the living dead, a corpse animated by evil powers; cf. “zombie”.

An LLM does not “learn” from texts, as a human does. An LLM steals the texts, misappropriates them, and strips them of their human authorship so wholly as to erase their authors from history. Thereupon, as parasitoids typically do, the LLMs kill their host: They collectively extinguish human souls by poisoning the well of human discourse, leaving culture as an empty shell.

This is the death of texts, the end of authorship, and the annihilation of the use of representations for humans to connect to each other.

I have called “AI” a “messianic, eschatological cult”.2 I have thought that its boosters are humans who so badly want a god, they’ll sacrifice the world to attempt constructing one. This is correct, but it is not the whole truth: They worship vampires.

It is said that to stop a vampire, one must drive a stake through its heart. In principle, perhaps I may settle for dd if=/dev/random of=... on all of the disks holding the vampires’ models.
