<span>Hi.  I want to talk to you.</span>  You generally—but most of all, those of you who have a hand in public interest litigation and/or constitutional scholarship.  My motives are not only theoretical, but also eminently practical.  And this is of sufficient import that whatever the chances may be, while I’m making a website, I’d better put this message out there.

To avoid telegraphing my intentions publicly, I will speak here only in general terms.

We stand now at a world-historical hour of decision.  No less than Enlightenment civilization is on the line—and on the brink of crashing into an abyss.  Free speech is the battle line without which all other battles are lost by default, for there can be no action without advocacy—no dissidents without the expression of dissent—no heresy of thought without blasphemy of words.

Yet free speech has been decaying for over a half-century.  Both the legal and practical boundaries of expression have slowly shrunk, as advocates of free speech have struggled with a fighting retreat.  Long have I wanted to *do something* about this—whilst I have done nothing of substantial impact.

Now, more than ever, it is the time to act on the principle that the best defense is attack.  Public-interest lawyers and orgs seem to have their hands full with their valiant last-ditch efforts to preserve some shreds of [free speech and other civil liberties](https://web.archive.org/web/20250926140607/https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/09/countering-domestic-terrorism-and-organized-political-violence/).  I applaud them, but that’s not my plan.

Instead of grasping for the *status quo ante*, I wish to *expand* the boundaries of expression.  I wish to challenge the decades-long trend that existed *before* Trump.  I am a self-declared radical, and I want to make my own Sixties—*my way*, without attempting futilely to duplicate by rote what has come before me.

If I need to fight for my rights, I seek not a rearguard defense, but victory at the free-speech Battle of Austerlitz.  In my wildest dreams, I aspire to be remembered after my death as if I’d been the radioactive-mutant three-way speech-lovechild of [Barney Rosset][rossetafter], [Stanley Fleishman][fleishmanpapers], and [William O. Douglas][douglaspoints].  Impossibly idealistic, and even grandiose as that may sound, I know that I need to aim high, if I wish to achieve anything at all.

The more intelligent Trumpists understand this; a decade ago, a faction of Trump’s most radical supporters had a slogan, “The Alt-Right is the new Left,” which was a direct shot at Sixties radicalism.  I, too, understand this.  And I believe that Trumpism cannot be defeated without an equal and opposite radicalism, born of fresh new ideas—and birthed in the expression thereof.

Reactionary politics by the liberal establishment cannot stop Trumpism.  The past decade’s experience endorses my conclusion about this.
