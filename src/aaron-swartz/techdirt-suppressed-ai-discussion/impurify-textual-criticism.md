Before I necessarily proceed to rebut this **at length,** I caution for future reference against the [the intentional fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intentional_fallacy).  To retcon your article to fit with your misreading of Schneier’s and Branch’s, you’re a hair’s breadth from expressly arguing what you or they allegedly *intended* to say—and then using the dirt-cheap propaganda tactic of attempting to enforce your implication with baseless *ad hominem* insults.

In the following, and as a general rule with few exceptions, I interpret a text by reading only what hermeneutists call “internal evidence” (or “public evidence”); this is similar to what lawyers call reading “within the four corners” of a document.  By this reading, neither the author(s) nor anyone else can claim that the text was intended to say other than what it says.

In *this* case, the *ad hominem* is non-fallacious—but it is nonetheless unproductive.

It’s more productive to point out three irreconcilable contradictions between Schneier’s and Branch’s position in the text I cited, and yours as it is expressed in this page’s article.  There are more, but I need not reach them.  Two are express contradictions; one is part express, part unavoidably implied.

But, when it comes to the AI fights, many are insisting that they want to do exactly that around AI[.]”  (The ungrammatical, self-contradictory disconnect of “control” and “exactly that” is not caused by my ellipsis; you do not write grammatically.)  This is in direct conflict with Schneier’s and Branch’s denunciation of the “AI” companies as “literally stealing”—and their whole text’s overarching effect of reconciling the open access movement with a legal crackdown on said “stealing”.

0. An integral part of Schneier’s and Branch’s “corporate capture” argument is about “control over training data *and infrastructure*” (my emphasis), in a process by which “public knowledge is absorbed into proprietary systems that the public cannot inspect, audit or meaningfully challenge”.  While you actively advocate for that absorption into proprietary systems, you totally ignore infrastructure.  Which leads me to...

0. You and they identify a problem in superficially similar, but substantively different ways:  **Your stated desire to “make sure that AI wasn’t *just* controlled by corporate juggernauts” (my emphasis) is *not* their “corporate capture” argument.**  By ineluctable logic, you and they then propose solutions that are not only different, but mutually exclusive.

Here is where your ignorance of the infrastructure problem kills your argument.  For “grassroots AI” to compete with companies who collectively have *trillions of dollars* worth of infrastructure is sheer fantasy.
