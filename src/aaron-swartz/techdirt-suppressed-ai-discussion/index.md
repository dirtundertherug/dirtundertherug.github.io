## Why? { #why }

Why am I troubling myself to restore this discussion to the Open Web?  Two reasons: (a)&nbsp;In conscience, for the same reasons stated in my first comment linked above; and, (b)&nbsp;this:

> “How long is it going to take before moderation system designers realize that the simple act of trying to repress intelligent, substantive discussion they don’t like online is likely to make it so that something that most people would *never, ever* see (like a few blog comments posted by an obscure person on a three-year-old <i>Techdirt</i> article) is now seen by many more people?  Let’s call it the Masnick Effect.” — <a href="https://dirtundertherug.com/about/impurify.html" class="impurify"><em>impurify</em></a>, closely paraphrasing [Mike Masnick (2005) from his coining of the term “Streisand Effect”][masnick-streisand] (emphasis is Masnick’s).

[masnick-streisand]: https://www.techdirt.com/2005/01/05/since-when-is-it-illegal-to-just-mention-a-trademark-online/ "Mike Masnick (2005-01-05). “Since When Is It Illegal To Just Mention A Trademark Online?” Techdirt."

[The singular comment that apparently was worthwhile for someone to suppress](impurify-textual-criticism.html) shall be on the Open Web, and it shall be on the Open Web *forever*.  So shall the whole discussion.

Although I intend one last comment on the *Techdirt* thread, which I’ll [POSSE](https://indieweb.org/POSSE) here, I will not continue the discussion in the dark.  If Mr. Masnick wishes to comment here, I promise to exercise a journalistic quality of ethics and editorial discretion in deciding what to publish—with full fairness to the right of reply by a subject to what, hereby, is guerrilla open press coverage.  My email address is in this page’s footer.

## And? { #and }

Beginning *immediately* upon my discovery that the discussion was suppressed, I started in haste to dash off this quick-and-dirty website.  It nonetheless took me about two days.

There are people who can spin up a new website in hours, or even minutes; they have their favored toolchains handy, and they have the infrastructure in place.  Those are dangerous people, from the perspective of anyone who wants to suppress a discussion.  *Thus far*, I have not been one of them.
