I was thinking of something else.  For some examples, please see this and the paper-listing “awesome” repos to which it links:  [Awesome Black Box Attacks in Adversarial Examples (Github)](https://github.com/KululuMi/Awesome-Black-Box-Attacks-in-Adversarial-Examples-).  We need powerful adversarial attacks for natural language text—preferably imperceptible to humans, as some image and audio attacks are.

Is it possible?  I am not an expert in this field.

For those who have websites, there are a bunch of open-source repos out there (not on Microsoft Github) with code for lightweight generation of poison webpages—hyperlink mazes of psychotic word salad that is syntactically correct, but semantically meaningless.  The hopeful idea is to feed LLM training scrapers garbage that can’t easily be filtered out, and that would reduce model quality while wasting a ton of costly GPU cycles.  *Make slop sloppier!*  Or at least, keep bots that ignore `robots.txt` busy scraping nonsense.  Nowadays, as I mentioned, even [Cloudflare offers this as an opt-in service on all of their plans](https://blog.cloudflare.com/ai-labyrinth/).

But to my awareness, these systems are not based on any rigorous adversarial machine learning research—and they are certainly not in the same class as the adversarial examples.
