Mr. Masnick, I observe that instead of taking on the stronger arguments from others, you first pick on a downright silly argument...

> But, man, that takes me back to people who used to make that EXACT SAME silly claim on my posts about file sharing two decades ago.

...and then, without any logical segue, you somehow jump from the benefits of sharing copies, [an argument embedded in the copyright discourse](https://dirtundertherug.com/copyright/plagiarism.html), into an overt apologia for plagiarism:

> \[...\] and even if whoever copies my work tries to hide my name on it, that would likely be to my advantage too. After all, smart people would (1) figure out the copier was hiding my name, damaging their reputation but (2) if not it would drive more interest in me and my ideas, and I’m quite able to profit from that.

That last sentence is <mark class="fragmark" id="so-poorly-written">so poorly written that it doesn’t even parse logically.  <mark class="fragmark" id="did-an-llm-write-it">Did an LLM write it?</mark></mark>

Is (2) supposed to say that if no one figured out that the *plagiarist* (not “copier”) was hiding your name, it would drive interest in you (“me and my ideas”)?  That’s the logical sentence-diagram reading, but it’s nonsensical—indeed, it’s as nonsensical as if you’d asked an LLM to “hallucinate” a bad argument for you.  It reminds me of something I once saw posted in a maths forum:  A stochastic parrot’s authoritative declaration that 85 is a prime number.

Or is (2) supposed to mean that it would drive interest in you if the copyist *didn’t* hide your name?  That makes perfect sense.  I totally agree.  I’d love for humans to share copies of my public writings with other humans, with proper attribution.  ♥️ *Sharing is caring.*  But that’s not what you said in this sentence!  It’s what you said in the portions which, for brevity, I elided from the above quotes.

By the by, (1) is empirically false in practice and wrong in principle.  “Smart people” shouldn’t need to do philological detective work to discover authorship—as I not infrequently attempt on today’s sick, sick Internet.  In fact, very few people even try.  And in fact, this does little to “damage the reputation” of both social media influencers and [“AI” companies who not only rip off an author’s work, but blatantly delete the author’s name from history](https://justine.lol/history/).

At this juncture, I must always remind the reader that *plagiarism* is worse than theft.  From its roots in classical antiquity, well over a millennium before copyright was invented, the word “plagiarism” is etymologically an ancient metaphor for **a crime of violence against the author.**  It’s literally *kidnapping*.
