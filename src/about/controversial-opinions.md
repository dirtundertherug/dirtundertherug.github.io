<span>Love me or hate me,</span> sing encomia unto me, or burn me in effigy, but let it be for what I am—not for what I am not.  Let me acquit myself by my words, or fall upon the sword of my pen—but my words are mine, [*mine*, mine by right][quasi-affidavit], not to be [socially devalued][social-inflation] as collateral damage of [cultural vampires run amok][llm-vampires], and surely not to be [stolen from me in sheer malice][defamation].

Whereas in defense of my words, and of *my right to my own words*, I am now in a predicament.

In the unluckiest of times, and by ill circumstance in the most unpremeditated way, I spoke up on the Internet to voice controversial opinions.  It is not for trouble’s sake; to the contrary, I yearn for the peaceful life that I have never had.  [I hold my opinions sincerely][why-controversial], as convictions formed after long thought and study.  Moreover, I am alarmed at the narrowing of the ranges of socially acceptable thoughts within my lifetime—even within the past twenty-five years!  I must do my part to resist this.  Overall, only by sheer indifference to all what I most claim to care about could I stay silent.

Were I denounced for my ideas, I could do no other than to quote [Justice Douglas][time1975]:  “These tracts may be the essence of wisdom to some; to others, their point of view and philosophy may be anathema.  To some ears, their words may be harsh and repulsive; to others, they may carry the hope of the future.”[^douglas1953]  As that passage continues, Douglas writes the U.S. Supreme Court’s first verbatim use of what is now a stock phrase, “the market place of ideas”.  Surely, I am entitled to nothing more than the opportunity to access that “market”.

To that end (and in truth, for all else in my life), I have but one means to reach people:  The written word, and whatever facility therefor I may be graced.

It is my only tool, my one currency—my sole weapon.  Without it, I can fight no battles at all—not even when, in my opinion, we stand now at an historic hour of decision, and Enlightenment civilization stands at the brink of collapsing into an abyss.

There is no more effective, nor crueler means to silence a writer than to steal from them *the usage of* their own writing.

Yet to fight for my only weapon, I risk compromising its use.  I must needs rally others who appreciate the import of words, and warn them of the subtler dangers to every aspiring author from the *cultural pollution* of <abbr title="Large Language Model" class="smallcap">LLM</abbr>s.  It is a cause greater than myself—not one I would have chosen, but one which chose me by accident of fate.

My individual case is unusual, yet representative of the ill ends which, in time, will foreseeably befall a whole class of creative minds who have not yet established their reputations; the unusualness of my case only makes me an apt canary in the creative coal mine, a test case of compound worst-case scenarios.  It is surely for the greater good that I must fight this to the finish—as, indeed, I have no choice but to do.  Yet to do so, I must connect with others in the first instance—without the social prejudices attendant to controversial opinions.

Thus do I find myself fighting a two-front war—where from risk of making enemies of some potential allies at my flank, I feel pressured to [throw away my weapons at my front][self-censorship].

This cannot be—not if the fabric of human discourse is to be preserved, such that the “marketplace of ideas” can continue to exist.  The best that I can do is honestly to explain the foregoing, and thereupon to repose my trust in the wisdom of those with whom I have common cause against so-called <abbr>“AI”</abbr>.[^so-called-ai]
